00 / This month

Less waiting. Cleaner numbers.

Six updates this month. Your team can add a database, schedule a job and open a shell without a ticket. ZopNight now shows Bedrock spend and keeps the monthly tax out of your alerts.

 

01 / Without a ticket

Add a database or a job yourself

Managed datastores: Add PostgreSQL, MySQL or Redis from the New menu, under Datastores. ZopDay sets up the password, port and storage for you. The database stays private to its environment, and its data stays until you delete it. On VM environments for now.

Scheduled jobs: Add a job from the New menu and run it hourly, daily, weekly, on your own cron expression, or once after each deploy. Turn on alerts and a failed run pings your team.

 

02 / One place to look

See an environment, then fix it from the browser

Environment overview: CPU, memory, failing services, the services using the most CPU and recent deploys, on one screen. A "Runs on" card shows the cloud, region and cluster or VM pool, so you know where you are before you act.

Browser terminal: Open a shell on a VM, or inside a service's container, from the console. No SSH keys to share: it connects through your cloud's own channel (Systems Manager on AWS, Run Command on Azure, IAP on Google Cloud). Admins have it by default, and every session goes in the audit log.

 

03 / AI spend

See what Bedrock costs. Stop mistaking tax for a spike.

Amazon Bedrock in AI Spend: Spend per model per day, including Claude billed through AWS Marketplace, plus calls, tokens, errors, latency and which identities call each model. It sits beside Azure OpenAI and your AI Gateway providers.

Tax pin: AWS posts a month's tax as one charge on the 1st. ZopNight now shows it as a Tax pin on that day, not a spike in your daily chart, and leaves it out of cost anomaly checks. The alerts you get are the ones worth reading.

 

Where to find them

Datastores, scheduled jobs, the environment overview and the browser terminal are in ZopDay. Bedrock spend and the tax pin are live in ZopNight.
